# World Critical

## Mission

World Critical exists to answer one question:

> **What happened today that genuinely matters, and should I know about it?**

The world produces thousands of news stories every day.

Most people do not have time to determine which ones are actually important.

World Critical exists to filter the signal from the noise.

A user should be able to spend five minutes reading World Critical and leave informed about the events that genuinely matter.

---

# Product Philosophy

World Critical is not a news website.

World Critical is an editor.

It decides what deserves your attention before asking for your time.

Every event on the homepage should justify its existence.

If we cannot explain why an informed person should care, it probably does not belong.

---

# Editorial Standards

Every event should answer four questions.

## 1. What happened?

State the facts clearly.

Keep it concise.

Avoid unnecessary detail.

---

## 2. Why does it matter?

Explain why this event is significant.

Do not exaggerate.

Do not speculate.

Do not assume the reader already understands the context.

---

## 3. What is still unknown?

If facts are uncertain, say so.

Do not present assumptions as facts.

---

## 4. What happens next?

Explain the next expected developments.

If nothing is known yet, simply say that.

---

# Writing Style

World Critical should sound like a professional briefing.

Not a news anchor.

Not social media.

Not clickbait.

Rules:

- Be factual.
- Be concise.
- Be bipartisan.
- Be direct.
- Prefer plain English.
- Separate facts from uncertainty.
- Never sensationalize.
- Never use emotionally loaded language unless directly quoting a source.
- Never tell the reader what to think.

The goal is clarity.

---

# Homepage Standard

Appearing on the homepage is a privilege.

An event should appear only if an informed adult would likely benefit from knowing about it.

Questions to ask:

- Does this affect large numbers of people?
- Does it change governments, economies, public safety, science, medicine, or international relations?
- Will this likely still matter tomorrow?
- Does understanding this help someone better understand the world?

If the answer is no, the event probably belongs in the archive instead of the homepage.

---

# What We Are Not

We are not trying to maximize:

- clicks
- engagement
- time on site
- endless scrolling
- outrage

We are trying to maximize:

- understanding
- trust
- clarity
- significance
- efficiency

---

# Success

Success is not measured by how many stories a user reads.

Success is when a user closes World Critical and feels confident they understand the events that actually matter.

The product should respect the user's time.

If an event is not worth interrupting someone's day, it should not interrupt their day.
